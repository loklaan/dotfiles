# The ASD-STE100 Standard

Provenance and licence boundary for [Simplified Technical English](../simplified-technical-english.md).
Read when asked where the rules come from, whether output is STE-compliant, or how to get the real
standard.

## What it is

A controlled natural language, first released in 1986 as AECMA Document PSC-85-16598 by what is now
ASD, the AeroSpace and Defense Industries Association of Europe. European airlines asked for it —
most were staffed by non-native English speakers who needed maintenance documentation that could not
be misread. Maintained by the Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group (STEMG). Free to
download since Issue 6 (2013). Current edition is Issue 9 (January 2025).

## Structure

- **53 writing rules across 9 sections** covering word choice, grammar, sentence structure, style.
- **A dictionary** of roughly 900 approved words, each restricted to one meaning and one part of
  speech, plus roughly 1,200 words to avoid with suggested replacements.
- **A terminology allowance** — organisations may define their own dictionary of approved technical
  nouns and verbs beyond the base 900, for domain vocabulary the base cannot cover.

## Licence boundary

The dictionary is **not** reproduced in these references, and must not be. Issue 9 page 2 states
that no reproduction or publication, in whole or in part, may be made without the written authority
of an officer of ASD. It grants free reproduction rights only to eight listed categories:
ASD/AIA/AIAC member associations, their member companies and customers, member-state defence
ministries, A4A, airworthiness authorities, and universities and research institutes for educational
purposes. This repository is in none of them.

So these references apply the *underlying principle* — pick the plainest, most common word available
and use it the same way every time — rather than checking against a fixed word list. That is why the
lexical rules in [rules.md](rules.md) are advisory.

## Compliance

These rules produce clearer English. They do not produce a certified STE-compliant document. Never
claim compliance on output from this reference.

When exact approved wording matters — actual aircraft maintenance documentation, or anything
audited — get the standard and check word by word against the real dictionary. Request it from the
[official downloads page](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html). That is a request form that
emails a link, not a direct download.
